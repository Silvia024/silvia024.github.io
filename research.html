Research

My research explores how people navigate, experience and interact in immersive environments, and how this understanding can inform adaptive streaming and collaborative XR systems.

Social Behaviour in Shared Virtual Spaces

How do people coordinate when they share a virtual space?

When people share a virtual space, they still have to settle where to look, how close to stand and who speaks next. I study how shared attention, interpersonal distance, turn-taking and collective decisions emerge in social XR.

Group experiences need group-level evaluation: our QoMEX 2025 Best Paper proposes shared mental models as a complement to individual quality of experience. Related work examines how social density shapes behaviour, and how narrative elements guide where people go in photorealistic social VR.

Behavioural Metrics for Interactive Systems

What can behaviour tell a system?

Movement, gaze, voice and decision timing trace how people experience an immersive system. I turn these signals into validated behavioural metrics, using information theory, clustering and machine learning, that systems and designers can act on.

Rather than treating behaviour as an output to observe, I treat it as an input to act on. Clustering 6-DoF navigation reveals users who behave alike, which no single data stream shows, and entropy-based measures indicate how predictable a user's motion is. I am first author of A Cookbook of User Metrics in Extended Reality (ACM Computing Surveys, under revision), a framework for choosing behavioural, physiological and self-reported metrics.

Adaptive Immersive Streaming

What if the user were at the heart of the streaming chain, not at the end of it?

Immersive systems must scale, adapt to each user, and stay within bandwidth and latency budgets. My work puts the interactive user at the heart of the coding, streaming and rendering chain.

Knowing how users behave lets a system spend bandwidth where people actually look. Our user-affinity metric (ACM TOMM 2020, Nicholas Georganas Best Paper Award) informed system-design strategies achieving up to 70% savings in encoding and storage costs while improving user experience. More recent work extends this to multi-party volumetric communication, from the open-source VR2Gather platform to WebRTC-based volumetric video conferencing.
